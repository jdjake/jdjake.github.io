Kindred are sentient folk divided into three types: Mortal, Fairy, or Demi-Fey. There are six playable options.

Mortals

Kindred from the mortal world. They suffer from Mortality, with a lifespan counted in decades, and suffer from all manner of diseases and ailments. All but a select few mortals have No Innate Magic, and most are mistrutful of magic, viewing it as perilous and unnatural. Most mortals are Religious, worshiping deities, exalted heroes, ancestors, and spirits, and those who dedicate themselves to the service of a religion may recieve blessings in the form of holy spells. They cluster together in large towns in The Dolmenwood for safety.

Human

As is the way in the wider world beyond The Forest, Humans are found throughout The Dolmenwood.

Most humans are adherents to the Pluritine Church, the dominant religion in Dolmenwood, and the official religion of the Duchy of Brackenwold. The Church worships a nameless, male god, referred to as The One True God. Daily practice focuses on venerating Saints, exalted people who performed miraculous deeds on behalf of the Church, now said to be intermediaries between Mortals and God. They believe naming or depicting God is heresy, that sentient life is sacred and must be protected and nurtured, that free will is fundamental yet evil deeds must be punished (in life and through eternal damnation in hell), and that God created the world of Fairy to demonstrate the folly of life without faith and salvation.

The old religions worshipping the Old Gods of The Wood have mostly been swept away, yet remnants of the worship of the old gods live on in folklore and tradition. For instance, many humans believe consuming mead and ale is a means of communion with greater powers, hearkening back to the worship of The Green Man, god of feast, ale, revelry, and hunting. Rumors say the witches of Dolmenwood still worship the Old Gods in secret.

Human adventurers are commonly found amongst all Classes. Yet rare is the human with enough connection to Fairy to become an Enchanter.

Mechanically, Humans tend to be more Decisive than other Kindred (humans act first on a tied initiative roll), exhibit Leadership more naturally than others (the loyalty rating of Retainers is increased by 1), and are Quick To Learn (+10% to XP Earned).

Breggle

A proud, thick-furred, stubborn Folk, sometimes called Goatfolk (or Hregl in their own tongue). Once the sole masters of the fertile hills and meadows of the High Wold in the Southwest Dolmenwood, the ancient Breggle noble houses rule alongside humans, swearing fealty to the Dukes of Brackenwold. Breggles often live alongside humans, in towns and hamlets and cities, sometimes intermarrying. In human towns, Breggle are treated with respect and caution, as the power of the Longhorn noble houses is known and feared.

They saw that the horns of a Breggle grow as they earn wealth and reknown, and the length of a Breggles horn indicates their social class: the shorthorns form the working class, and the longhorns the noble class. Theoretically, a wealthy shorthorn’s horns may grow to the size of a longhorn, but acceptance in the ruling class is another matter.

As subjects of Duke Brackenwold, Breggles are nominally adherents to the Pluritine Church. True devotion is rare, however, as Breggles tend to offer up prayers to esteemed ancestors from their long history, a practice they call Aud frengd Hlerr, roughly translating to ‘Praising the Ancestors’. Aud frengd Hlerr teaches that the worlds of Mortal and Fairy are but testing grounds, and those that honor their families gather in an afterlife of eternal feating, whereas those that lead craven and spiritless lives enter a dismal realm of torment, where there souls are annihilated by gnawing worms.

Breggle adventurers are most commonly fighters, knights, or magicians. They seldom seek membership of the Pluritine Church as clerics or friars, and only very rare individuals have enough connection with Fairy to become Enchanters.

Mechanically, Breggle are Furry (+1 AC when unarmored or wearing light armor), and have Horns (which they can use to make attacks with). Longhorns may use their Intense Gaze to force humans and Shorthorns into submission.

Mosslings

Mosslings are a gnarled and woody Shortfolk native to Dolmenwood, gathering in small, isolated communities in the dark, dank forests of The Dolmenwood, burrowing their homes into the damp earth or inhabiting natural caves. They are stocky, pudgy, with green, yellow, or brown skin, textured like wrinkled bark and patched with mould. Mosslings become wiser and more plant-like as they age. After death, even their bones rot away to mould. Their body decays to a rich compost within a year.

Mossling adventurers are commonly Fighters or Hunters. They are only ocassionally accepted into the ranks of Dolmenwood nobility as Knights, seldom seek membership in the Pluritine Church as Clerics or Friars, and rarely possess enough connection with Fairy to become Enchanters.

Mechanically, Mosslings have a Skill Target of 5 for Survival when Foraging, and are Resilient, having a +4 Bonus to Saving Throws against fungal spores or poisons, and a +2 bonus to all other Saving Throws. As a Mossling ages, their dank, fertile flesh picks up seeds and spores germinating into Symbiotic Flesh, which gives them a new trait at each level. Mosslings also know a carefully guarded, quasi-magical craft called a knack.

Fairies

Kindred from the realm of Fairy. Fairies are Immortal; they do not age, suffer from non-magical disease, or die naturally, though they may be killed. They are Innately Magical, natives of a world where mortal logic holds no sway. Unless disguised, Fairies are Unearthly; animals are invariably unnerved, and most Mortals will have never met a fairy, and percieve them with awe, fear, and caution. Fairies are Non-Religious; Fairies have no gods, and have no spiritual connection to mortal gods. Instead Fairy societies have myriad cosmologies and philosophies of living. Fairies are wracked with passions deeper than Mortals can fathom. They can be at once wise with measure and petty as children, with an unwavering devotion to their personal whims.

As the basest of all metals, the rarefied bodies of Fairies are burned by the touch of Cold Iron (Pure Iron), and suffer additional damage when hit by weapons made from such weapons. The touch of silver is repulsive to Fairies, instantly bringing on a queasiness of the stomach. They are Highly Resistant to Magic, gaining a +2 Magic Resistance.

Most Fairies never venture into the mortal world, but the more romantic, obsessive, or adventurous may gain a taste for the place. Fairy PCs have chosen to come to the mortal world, and Players should consider their reason for departure when creating Fairy characters (for example, in exile, fleeing some peril, or simply wishing for a new start).

Elves

As humans dominate the Mortal World, Elves do so in Fairy, forging vast kingdoms, subjugating others under their rule, and delving deeply into the secrets of magic. Elves are physically similar to humans but vary widely in apperance, with features such as pointed ears, small horns, or star shaped pupils. It is always possible to identify an elf for they carry an air of unearthliness about them (unless disguised by magic). Many kinds of elves in the boundless reaches of Fairy: the Pale-Elves of The Lightless Realms, the dark skinned, the Crystal-Rimed Elves of The Cloud Palaces, and the cold-hearted Frost-Elves who serve the wicked Prince of Winter, among countless others.

Elves enjoy being addressed by curious epithets hinting at their True Names in the tongue of Fairy (which they never reveal to Mortals). Elves who frequent Dolmenwood sometimes take on a nickname (a shortened version of their epithet) or a Mortal name, for convenience.

Elves who wander into the Mortal World may be fascinated with Mortal Kindred, with their short, spirited lifespans and inevitable aging and death. Some elves view Mortal company as peculiar and entertaining, while others earnestly seek to comprehend the Mortal mindset. Elves tend to be on good terms with other Demi-Fey and Fairies.

Elves are commonly Enchanters, Fighters, Hunters, or Magicians. It is rare for an Elf to be accepted into the Dolmenwood nobility as a Knight. Elves cannot be Clerics or Fighters as they have no spiritual connection with Mortal Gods.

Mechanically, Elves have a Skill Target of 5 for Listen and Search. They possess a minor magical talent known as a Glamour. They posesses an Unearthly Beauty which gives them +2 to Charisma when interacting with Mortals.

Grimalkin

Shape-shifting, Cat-Fairies reknowned for their Illusion Magic and love of eating rats. They originate in the Fairy realm of Catland, ruled by the fearsome Queen Abyssinia, the Queen of All Cats. Normally, Grimalkin take Estray Form, appearing as a humaonid cat, wearing clothing, speaking, and walking upright. In Chester Form, a Grimalkin resembles a normal cat in all aspects, though perhaps a little fatter; they can understand language, but only respond in yowls and meows. In Wilder Form, a Grimalkin is difficult to percieve, appearing only as a gleaming pair of deranged, predatory eyes. In this form they are at their most Primal and Fey, indiscriminately attacking friend and foe.

Grimalkins who wander in the Mortal World tend to become jealous and furtive among their own kind, so tend to prefer the company of other Kindred. They mostly regard the earnest undertakings of Mortals as comical and absurd. They are greeted with curiosity and wonder in human settlements, perhaps due to Human’s affection for domestic cats.

Grimalkin are commonly bards, enchanters, hunters, or thieves. It is rare for a Grimalkin to be accepted into the ranks of Dolmenwood Nobility to become a Knight. Grimalkin cannot be Clerics or Friars as they have no spiritual connection with Mortal Gods.

Mechically, Grimalkin are Small, and must wear armor tailored to their small size, and cannot wield Large weapons, but gain a +2 to AC in melee with large creatures. They can Hunt Rats to gain HP. They possess a minor magical talent known as a Glamour. They have a Skill Target of 5 for Listen. They can spend a round to Shapeshift into Chester Form freely, and into Wilder Form once per day.

Demi-Fey

Kindred with origin in the realm of Fairy that have taken up permanent residence in the mortal world. Over generations, exposure to the mortal world has changed demi-fey. They are Mortal, but with a lifespan of centuries. They have Remnants of Magic, diminished by long exposure to the mortal world. Demi-Fey are Superstitious. They have no spiritual connection to mortal Gods, instead accumulating odd beliefs and lore since arriving in the mortal world and derived from esteemed fairy ancestors.

Demi-Fey retain their ancestor’s vulnerability to Cold Iron, but have grown accustomed to silver.

Woodgrue

Woodgrues are capricious, bat-faced goblins who, many generations ago, forsook their ancestral home in Fairy and migrated to the dells of the Mortal World. They have massive, flapping ears and soft, downy fur upon their heads and chests, while the rest of their body appears like that of a human child.

Woodgrues live a nomadic lifestyle, wandering Dolmenwood alone or in small groups, following where their whims and nose lead. They love wild dancing and cacophonous music. The magic of Fairy lingers in the music of Woodgrues, for their songs can enchant folk to frolic into the deep woods. Woodgrues delight in using this magic for mischief and high jinks. While mischievous and rascally, woodgrues only cause serious harm if slighted.

In human settlements, woodgrues may be met with caution, as folks are aware of their rambunctious nature. However, it is known that barring woodgrues entry would likely only lead to greater misfortune.

Woodgrue adventurers are commonly bards, magicians, or thieves. It is rare for a woodgrue to be accepted into the ranks of the Dolmenwood nobility as a knight. Woodgrues cannot be clerics or friars as they have no spiritual connection with the deities of mortals.

Mechanically, Woodgrues are Small, and must wear armor tailored to their small size, and cannot wield Large weapons, but gain a +2 to AC in melee with large creatures. They are Compelled to Jubilate; if witnessing a celebration they are utterly compelled with every fibre of their being to partake (they may resist with a Save Versus Spell, but feel drained and downtrodden for the length of the engagement). Once per day, a woodgrue may play an enchanted Mad Revelry on a wind instrument while hooting and dancing terribly, with various results. A Woodgrue can see in the darkness up to 60’, viewing the world as if glowing with a faint mooonlight (fine detail cannot be percieved). And Woodgrues have a Skill Target of 5 for Listen.
