Session 5

14th October, 2026

Players

  • Ben (Playing Lord Mogglin)
  • Daz (Playing ?)
  • Jaco (Playing Nokko)
  • Kai (Playing Ser Garfunkel)
  • Ross (Playing ?)
  • Mungo (Playing Sprigrot)

XP Earned:

353 XP (Exploration)
360 XP (Encounters)
615 XP (Treasure)

1337 XP Each

Summary

Frisk the 12th of Reedwyrme

Weather is Cloudy and Misty

  • While the town rested to Prigwort, the party became aware of the Bounder’s, a gang of local youths who don’t take kindly to strangers, on grounds that they might be spies for the Nag-Lord who dwells in the Woods to the North.
    • The group decided to get involved. While Nokko and Lord Mogglin performed for an evening, the rest of the group kept an eye out for the gang around town. They found a small group of Bounders, lead by a pudgy, blonde-bearded man named Austache, roughing up a group of Breggle travellers passing through the town.
    • Lord Mogglin attempted to enchant Austache, but failed. Not taking to being charmed, the group became aggressive to the party, giving the Breggle travellers time to escape. Garfunkel and Sprigrot managed to intimidate the group with their magical swords, then Nokko enchanted the group with a song. The Bounder’s followed Nokko to the Gaol in town, got them to confess to their crimes (they even killed some folks!). They were then locked up for the night.

Eggfast the 13th of Reedwyrme

Weather is Frosty and Chill

  • In the morning, the party went to the Goal to check on the Bounders.
    • The Captain of the Guards, a bristle-bearded goggle-eyed man named Hogwash, told the party they’d been freed in the morning. They unconfessed to their crimes, claiming an enchantment, and were freed.
    • Most of the Bounders are bored Prigwort youths from the better-off families in town. They’ve got connections so the law mostly can’t get to them without solid evidence. Austache himself is a great-nephew of Sespretta and Blino Jongle, proprieters of the Clashed Antler of the Noble Brewing House Cobsworth.
    • Captain Hogwash wants to shut down the Bounders, but needs hard evidence of crimes.

  • In town, ‘Mother’ and some of her daughters sell pastries in the town square. ? (Dazz’s Character) bought a pastry, and flirted with Mother’s daughters. The party thought about rushing back to Mother’s house to investigate while she was in town, but decided not to

  • The group headed South of Prigwort, in an attempt to find the herbs in the gardens of the ancient stone house that the Alchemist Wrymspittle had heard rumors about.
    • Just to the south of Prigwort, in a forest of hunter’s crossed by small rivulets and clear, still pools, the group found a great tangle of hazel and holly concealling a deep, narrow, 60’ chasm. Dropping enchanted fire down, the party saw a small stream trickling down a narrow cave passage.
    • Further south, the party found themselves in a gloomy, indigo-shadowed wood. The sounds of croaking frogs and toads surrounded them. The older trees all had consistent looping perfect-spiraleld burl about halfway up its length, which was quite uncanny.
      • In the center of a hex, the party found a clearing containing a small, unsupported arch of ancient stone, covered in lichen. Crawling underneath the arch did not appear to do anything.
      • The party searched the hex, finding a forest of willow trees and sodden ground on the encroachment of Hag’s Addle. A narrow cave mouth stood amidst a pile of great boulders, with a faint green glow coming from within, and a heady, earthern aroma.
  • In the same hex as the whorled trees, the arch, and the cave, the party found the icy stone house. Frost clung to the old stone walls, like the hunters of Prigwort said. Undeterred, the party approached the house, hoping to find the herb and whatever other valuables might have survived the centuries of abandonment.
    • In the back of the house, in a small herb garden under a frost-rimed tree, and beside a well frozen solid with a thick layer of ice, the party found the herb garden. There were only three usable herbs, which the party bundled with their Sylvan signs, to prevent misidentification.
    • Once inside, the party began exploring and looting the house, searching its rooms for anything of value or interest. The hearth was frozen over, though a flute hung from the mantle its dining table set for two, plates hand-painted with tree-branches and labelled “Beak-and-Cradle” and “Thin-and-Bone’ in Sylvan. A faint weeping could be heard from within the house.
    • In a child’s bedroom, Ross found a small bed, faded clothes, and a toy chest containing Sylvan letter blocks, a felt pony, a mitten, and a child’s note, in mispelled Sylvan, promising to return home: “Mama, I promise I will come back”; accompanied is a drawing of a waterfall flowing into two diverging rivers.
    • Ross searched the other bedroom, disturbing a ghostly figure, with long silver hair down to her shins, and a ruffled white dress. She wept for her daughter, pleading Ross to return her, getting angrier and angrier, until she attacked. Ser Garfunkel attacked with his magical starlit sword, eventually entering Wilder form in an attempt to hold her off while the others searched the house.
    • Meanwhile, Mogglin and Nokko raided the kitchen. It’s hearth was smashed, cookware and crockery lay destroyed, and a skeleton remained with a rusted spear thrust through its ribcage. Four famine-wrecked living corpses, still clad in rusted mail and tattered blue-and-black hoods, stood, alert to the sounds of the wraith pleading for her daughter in the other room; the pair managed to close the door before the ghouls noticed them. Sprigrot put a stone chair over the door to prevent the Ghouls from entering the dining room for a time.
    • Lord Mogglin saw a glint of gold in the ice of the well. Working with Dazz, they built a fire to melt the ice, finding a golden locker within. Inside, a tiny painted portrait of a golden-haired elven child. At the same time, Garfunkel banished the wraith, which cursed him as he died, while Sprigrot and Debbian fought off the ghouls that had broken through from the kitchen.
    • Once clear of the undead, the group finished raiding the house. They found a journal, written in Sylvan, a diary of Thin-and-Bone’s everyday life. It described her worry for her child Beak-and-Cradle, her pride for her child’s gift of music, and her plans to trade with the Elven towers to the west. The final entry reads “Soldiers of Men on the road. We will be fine. We are hidden, and no-one of consequence”.
