The Basic Game Procedure

  1. The Referee Describes the situation, depicting the environment, characters, hazards, and objects present, and noting any smells, sounds, or other features.
  2. The players ask Questions to clarify description elements.
  3. The players put themselves in their characters shoes, discussing what to do, and then Declaring Actions they wish to perform. This can be anything imaginable, from speaking to characters present, interacting with the environment, or using a special capability granted to them by their Kindred or Class. The Referee clarifies potential risk involved with such actions. Each Character may perform a different action, though it usually beneficial for a Party to stick together and act in concert with one another.
  4. The Referee Judges the potential results of the proposed action. Specific game rules may apply, and an appropriate Resolution System may apply, or the Referee will come up with a ruling based on the specific situation. The Referee presents the odds to a player, making the likelihood of success and failure and potential risks clear. The player may then alter their approach, or come up with alternate plans to tip the odds in their favor.
  5. The action is Resolved, potentially involving some kind of dice roll. Then Time Passes, depending on the action taken. Time is normally divided into discrete segments called Rounds (10 Seconds) and Turns (10 minutes). In hazardous environments, a Wandering Monster roll may be made to determine if the Party encounters something by chance.

Resolution Systems

Skill Checks

Some actions require passing a Skill Check to succeed. All adventurers can use the Listen, Search, and Survival skills. Some classes grant the use of additional, specialized skills. To make a Skill Check, roll a d6, and add any situational modifiers. If the roll equals or exceeds the character’s Skill Target, they succeed in the Skill Check, and the action they carry out succeeds. A natural 1 always fails and a natural 6 always succeeds.

Ability Checks

An Ability Check represents a test of a character’s physical or mental aptitudes (one of their Ability Scores) rather than a particular learned skill. Roll a d6 and add the Ability Modifier of the relevant Ability. If the result equals or exceeds a target number of 4, the check succeeds. A natural 1 always fails and a natural 6 always succeeds.

Saving Throws

Certain dangerous or detrimental effects call for a Saving Throw, or Save, which is a roll to determine whether a character avoids the effect. There are 5 Save Categories, each for a different type of hazard. Roll a d20 and add applicable modifiers. If the result equals or exceeds the character’s Save Target for the relevant Save Category, the Saving Throw Succeeds.

Doom are effects that instantly kill or inflict ongoing physical malignancies, such as poison, disease, and death magic. Ray are effects coming from rapidly moving, directional effects, such as lightning bolts. Hold are effects that hinder or prevent movement, such as paralysis or falling rocks that may crush or pin characters. Blast are effects that fill a wide area, such as explosions. Spell applies to potent, directly targeted effects, such as arcane or holy spells.

In situation not covered by the rules, the Referee should run down the categories listed above, choosing the first that fits the effect in question. If an effect does not fit in any category, default to Doom.

Attack Rolls

This roll is used to determine whether an attacker lands a blow on an opponent. Roll a d20 and add any applicable modifiers (your Attack bonus, as well as your Strength Modifier for Melee Attacks, and Dexterity Modifier for Ranged Attacks). If you beat or exceed the defender’s Armor Class, you succeed.

Luck Check

Used when the outcome of a situation depends on pure chance. The Referee proposes a chance of success, and a relevant dice is rolled to determine if the Luck Check succeeds.

Example Resolutions

  • If players wish to make a reasonable climb (e.g. climbing a tree) in a non-pressured situation, no Check may be required. In more difficult or tense situations, a Dexterity Ability Check may be required to succeed, with a Failure resulting in the Character falling halfway through making the climb.

  • A stuck door that is unlocked but so obstructed that it cannot be opened without force may be shoulder barged with a successful Strength Ability Check. The Referee should probably warn the player if this will produce a loud noise, which may attract attention to the Party. Similarly, a Locked door may be broken open with an axe or crowbar with a successful Strength Ability Check. Alternatively a Locked Door may be picked by a thief using a Pick Lock Skill Check, or open via a magic spell (likely with no Check required).

  • When two groups approach one another, there is a chance one or the other will be unaware of the other’s presence. This is handled with a Surprise Roll, which is a Luck Check for each group, with odds depending on the vigilence of the other party.

Encounter Procedures

Encounter Procedures apply when adventurers meet strangers, foes, or monsters in the wild.

  1. The Referee determines the Awareness of each party of one another. This applies if a group is making a lot of noise, or carrying a light in the dark.
  2. The Referee rolls for Surprise, if one party is not aware of the other. The standard chance of being surprised is 2-6, but may be modified in certain situations.
  3. The Referee determines the Distance between the two groups when encountered.
  4. The Referee determines the Initiative, i.e. which side acts first. Each side rolls a d6, the highest roll determining whom goes first.
  5. Both Sides declare their intended Actions. If one side decides to start attacking the other side, begin following Combat Procedures. If one side wishes to avoid the encounter, it may attempt to Flee the encounter. A side may attempt to Parley with the other, and provided combat has not been initiated, a Charisma bonus may determine a Monster or NPC Reaction to the party.

Combat Procedures

  1. Both sides declare their Intended Actions. The intention to cast a spell must be declared, because the spell is disrupted if the caster loses initiative and is harmed. The intention to flee from melee must be declared, for opponents that win initiative against a fleeing combatant are able to attack their opponents with a bonus.
  2. Each side rolls d6 to determine Initiative, who acts first.
  3. Each side, in order, moves and attacks. Combatants may not move while casting spells.
  4. The Referee makes a Morale Check for NPCs, if applicable.

Alternate Alignment System

Law and Chaos are diametrical cosmic forces in the universe. The tension between Law and Chaos give rise to the very threads from which gods, fairy, mortals, and reality itself is woven. Law is frequently associated with ‘Goodness’, Chaos with ‘Evil’, but these are generalizations, not rules. Neither are truly benevolent or malevolent.

All beings have an Alignment which represents a character’s orientation on the cosmic scale, the ratios of the cosmic threads from which a person’s soul is woven. In practice, there are infinite ways by which a being’s soul may be tied to the universe, as many ways as there are styles to braid the cosmic threads together. But for the purposes of game mechanics, beings are categorized under one of three alignments: Law, Neutrality, or Chaos.

Alignments have nothing to do with a character’s allegiances, personality, or morality; whether or not a character with either of these alignments is actively engaged in the Cosmic Struggle, their souls are marked with a cosmic affiliation nonetheless. Animals and other creatures of lesser sentience tend to be neutral, but not always. Characters of Neutral Alignment are also embodiments of Law and Chaos, but these exist in a more balanced state within the character’s being than those of Lawful or Chaotic alignment. Neutrality is not a cosmic force in the universe.

Player characters are free to select whichever Alignment they choose, though Clerics must be Lawful, and Friars cannot be Chaotic. GM’s should not use Alignment as a tool to police the behavior of aligned characters. But there are powerful beings and institutions within The Dolmenwood with the ability to do so, as they entwine fates, drawing aligned characters to their banner’s cause. Because Alignments represent fundamental forces of the universe, a character’s Alignment may predicate very real mechanical effects and consequences as they interact with the magic, monsters, and more powerful beings within The Dolmenwood.

Law

Law is the force of order, stability, and harmony, but also stagnation, imprisonment, and fascism. The Cosmic Struggle of Law is to ensure the events of the world inevitably march on their flawless, unchanging plans towards the ultimate, irrefutable truth of the universe. As time moves on, all distraction and resistance to this plan will falter, until everything will be in its perfect state forevermore, without alteration or the possibility of possibilities. Those who are Lawful in Alignment are part of this inevitable destiny, awash on the turbulent seas of fate, but often have little knowledge of what that destiny is and what their role will be in fulfilling it. So those that pursue the Cosmic Struggle of Law are forever looking for signs and omens to show them their proper way.

Chaos

Chaos is the force of change and freedom, but also destruction, indifference, and lawlessness. Chaos is the howling maelstrom beyond the veil of shadows and existence. It bends and tears the fabric of the universe; it destroys all that seeks to be permanent. It allows great miracles as reality alters at the whim of those that can call upon the eldritch forces, and it causes great catastrophe as beings we call demons (and far, far worse) rip into our reality and lay waste to all. Everything that is made will be unmade. Nothing exists, and nothing can ever exist, not in a way that the cosmos can ever recognize. Those who pursue the Cosmic Struggle of Chaos consider the world in terms of ebbing and flowing energy, of eternal tides washing away the sand castles that great kings and mighty gods build for themselves. To be Chaotically Aligned is to be an attractor to higher beings and cosmic forces in need of pawns in the pursuit of needless goals. Many who are aligned with Chaos desperately wish they were not.

Neutrality

To be Neutral is merely to exist between the forces of Law and Chaos. Most beings in the universe are born with Neutral Alignment, and remain so unless drastic measures (often unwittingly followed) realign the cosmic threads of their existence.

In the real world, every human being that has ever existed has had Neutral Alignment; Law and Chaos are fantastical supernatural forces unlike any of us have experienced in our mundane lives. Examples of the most Lawfully Aligned characters are those deeply bound with the concept of fate, whether they are fulfilling a predetermined destiny or attempting to escape it. Such figures include Luke Skywalker, King Arthur, Paul Atreides, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, or The Antichrist. The most Chaotically Aligned characters are granted supernatural powers to change the world as they will it, or characters which always seem to run into destruction or misfortune. Examples of Chaotic characters include Superman, most wizards, and almost all of The Muppets.
